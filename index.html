Palette The 8 weeks

A beginner's watercolour practice

Mizu

Eight weeks, one skill at a time. Every week pairs a short lesson with a highly-rated video, so you spend your time painting instead of hunting for what to learn next.

8 weeks 1 new skill each week 1 curated video per week 20–40 min a session

How this works

Mizu isn't a full course — it's a weekly nudge. Read the focus, watch the video, then paint the practice prompt on scrap paper before you worry about a finished piece.

01

One skill, all week

Each week isolates a single technique. Repeating it on scrap paper matters more than any single "good" painting.

02

Follow the bloom

Watercolour behaves on its own terms. Watch what the paint does before trying to control it — that's most of the skill.

03

Keep a sampler sheet

Save every practice swatch on one sheet of paper. By week 8 it becomes a reference chart of everything you've learned.

A starter palette

The 8-week plan

Work through them in order — each week leans on the one before it. If a week feels shaky, stay another week before moving on.

1

Week 1 — Foundations

Brush control & water-to-pigment ratio

Before technique, get comfortable with your tools. This week is about how much water is too much, how a brush behaves fully loaded versus nearly dry, and what your particular paper and paint can do.

This week's practice

Fill a page with strokes at four water levels — dry, damp, wet, and dripping — and label each one.

Week 2 — Wash control

Flat, graded & variegated washes

A wash is watercolour's basic unit — a smooth, even layer of colour across a shape. This week covers the three core washes and the water-control habits (like maintaining the "bead") that keep them from streaking.

This week's practice

Paint a sampler sheet: one flat wash, one graded wash from dark to light, one variegated wash blending two colours.

Week 3 — Wash control

Wet-on-wet

Painting onto paper that's already damp lets colour spread and bloom on its own — it's the technique behind watercolour's signature soft edges and is where most of the medium's "magic" comes from.

This week's practice

Wet a circle of paper with clean water, then drop in two or three colours and watch how they merge without touching them.

4

Week 4 — Wash control

Wet-on-dry & layering

The opposite approach: painting onto dry paper for crisp, controlled edges, then glazing more dry layers on top once each one has fully dried to build up depth without muddying colour.

This week's practice

Paint three overlapping circles in the same colour, letting each one dry completely before adding the next.

Week 5 — Colour

Colour mixing & the colour wheel

With water control in hand, turn to colour. Learn to mix the full rainbow from just three primaries, and start noticing which of your pigments run warm or cool.

This week's practice

Build a 12-step colour wheel using only your red, yellow and blue.

Week 6 — Texture

Texture & special effects

Salt, splatter, and lifting turn a flat wash into something with grain and surprise. These effects are unpredictable by nature, so this week is about experimenting freely rather than aiming for a specific result.

This week's practice

Paint three washes at different wetness levels and sprinkle salt on each while still damp — compare the results once dry.

Week 7 — Light

Value, light & shadow

Value — how light or dark something is — does more to describe form than colour does. Working in a single pigment this week removes the distraction of colour so you can focus purely on tone.

This week's practice

Paint a five-step value scale in one colour, then use it to shade a simple sphere with a clear light source.

8

Week 8 — Putting it together

A loose floral, start to finish

Everything so far — washes, wet-on-wet, mixed colour, value — comes together in one small, forgiving subject. Loose florals don't need a pencil sketch or precise petals, which makes them a kind first "real" painting.

This week's practice

Paint a small bouquet in one sitting, no pencil first — let the brush decide the petal shapes as you go.
